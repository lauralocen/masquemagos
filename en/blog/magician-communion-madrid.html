Magician for a communion in Madrid: when to book, how it fits the banquet and price

November 2026 · 7 min read

In Madrid, most First Communions take place over a handful of weekends in May, and the best restaurants, estates and entertainers are booked months in advance. If you are organising your son's or daughter's, a magician is one of the best ways to keep the children entertained and give the star of the day their moment. Here is everything you need to know.

When communions take place in Madrid

The season runs from late April to early June, peaking on Saturdays and Sundays in May. Many parishes announce their dates in autumn, and that is when restaurant and estate bookings begin.

So the best time to book the magician is as soon as you have the parish date and the banquet venue. May Saturdays are the first to fill up.

Where they are held: restaurant, estate or home

  • Restaurant with a private room: the most common option in the city. The magician can do table-to-table magic or a show in one area of the room.
  • Estate or venue outside the city: very common in the north and the sierra. There is usually outdoor space for children to play, and the show works very well outdoors or in a marquee.
  • At home or in a residential complex: more intimate. The magician adapts to the living room or garden.

In every case, all the magician needs is a clear area where the children can sit at the front and see well.

When in the banquet to schedule the magician

This is the key point. A communion banquet lasts several hours and children can't stay seated the whole time. The best moment is usually:

  • Between the main course and dessert. The children have eaten and are starting to get up. The show gathers them and lets the adults finish in peace.
  • While the adults have coffee. The children go off to watch the magic and the grown-ups can chat.
  • Never at the start. If the magician performs before the meal, the children come to the table over-excited and lunch gets complicated.

Show or table-to-table magic

A show for the children

A one-hour show where the communion child is the star. It works with groups of children of different ages and is usually what everyone remembers most.

Close-up magic for the adults

The magician visits the tables with cards, coins and guests' own objects. Ideal while waiting between courses, and grandparents love it.

Many families combine both: a spell of table-to-table magic followed by the children's show.

How to give the star of the day their moment

  • Bring them on stage. The communion child takes part in the main routine and gets the big finale.
  • A personal touch. Their name appearing, the communion date on a card, something that is theirs.
  • A photo to remember. Tell the photographer when the show is on: some of the best photos of the day come from it.

What the magician needs

  • A clear area at the front, in the room, on the terrace or in the garden.
  • Space for the children to sit on the floor or on low chairs.
  • Sound: we bring it, included.
  • The approximate banquet schedule, so we arrive in good time.

Common communion mistakes (and how to avoid them)

  • Booking the magician in the last week. In May, many dates have been taken since winter.
  • Not telling the restaurant. Let them know there will be a show and when, so they keep an area free and it doesn't clash with dessert service.
  • Putting the magician in a separate room with no adults nearby. Children enjoy it more when they see their parents watching too.
  • Forgetting the youngest. If there are cousins aged 3 or 4, let us know: the show is adapted so they aren't left out.

Ideas families love

  • A magic keepsake. A card signed by the communion child to keep as a memento.
  • The finale photo. The child with the magician right after the last routine.
  • Grandparents as volunteers. Few things make children laugh more than seeing grandad amazed.

Tips for a stress-free communion

  • Book in order: first the parish, then the restaurant or estate, and straight after that the magician.
  • Count the children. Knowing how many children there will be and their ages helps tailor the show.
  • Plan a corner for them. A children's table near the show area makes everything much easier.
