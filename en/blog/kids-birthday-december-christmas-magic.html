A child's birthday in December: how to celebrate it with magic during the holidays

November 2026 · 6 min read

Having a birthday in December has its charm, but also its challenge. Between school Christmas shows, family lunches and holidays, it is easy for the birthday to get hidden or merged with the Christmas presents. If your son or daughter has a birthday at this time of year, here are ideas to give their party its own moment, and make it the one their friends remember.

The problem with a Christmas birthday

Children born in December know it well: sometimes the party gets postponed, mixed with another celebration or reduced to a quick snack because "there's already so much going on". And many friends can't come because they are away on holiday.

The good news is that with a bit of planning you can throw a birthday that stands out precisely because it is different.

Choose the date carefully

  • Before the school holidays. The first two weekends of December are the best: all their school friends are in town and the family lunches haven't started yet.
  • Avoid the 24th, 25th, 31st and 1st. Even if the birthday falls on those days, a party with friends works better on another date.
  • In January, after Epiphany. If the birthday is at the end of December, celebrating in the second week of January also works well: children come back to school ready for a party.

Where to hold a winter party

In December the park is not an option, so it is home or a venue:

  • At home: more intimate and affordable. It works well with 8 to 15 children if you have a clear living room.
  • At a venue: better for large groups or the whole class. Many venues fill up in December, so book early.

The magician adapts to both. All they need is a clear area at the front and space for the children to sit on the floor and see well.

Why a magician works so well at this time of year

In December children have lots of stimulation: lights, presents, school shows. For their birthday to stand out, it needs something they don't get every day. A magic show does that for several reasons:

  • The birthday child is the star. They come up on stage, do magic in front of their friends and get the big finale.
  • It keeps the whole group focused. An hour-long show where everyone takes part, with no fights or boredom.
  • It can have a festive touch. If you like, the show includes winter or Christmas details while still being a birthday.
  • Parents enjoy it too. And in the middle of the holiday season, that is welcome.

How to plan the afternoon

A simple structure for a two-and-a-half-hour party:

  • Arrival and free play (20-30 minutes) while everyone arrives.
  • Magic show (1 hour) once the whole group is there.
  • Snack and cake (30-40 minutes). After the show the children are hungry and happy.
  • Presents and goodbyes.

Invitations and dates: book early

December is one of the busiest months of the year for magicians and venues. If you already have the date, book as soon as you can. Send invitations at least two weeks ahead so families note it down before their diaries fill up with Christmas plans.

Ideas for a winter touch

Without turning it into a Christmas party, you can use the season to give the birthday a special feel:

  • Winter invitations. Snowflakes, stars or magician's hats. That way the birthday has its own look and isn't confused with Christmas cards.
  • A warm snack. Hot chocolate, decorated biscuits or homemade sponge cake. In December it beats fizzy drinks.
  • A cake that isn't Christmas-themed. Let the birthday child choose. It's their day, not Father Christmas's.
  • A little keepsake for guests. A magic wand, a small pack of cards or an easy trick to take home fits perfectly with the show.

Common mistakes with December birthdays

  • Combining it with the family Christmas lunch. Children want to celebrate with their friends. Two separate moments work better.
  • Inviting too late. If invitations arrive a week before, many families already have plans.
  • Not booking the venue first. Venues fill up early in December. Confirm the space before sending invitations.
  • Leaving the magician until the end. If the show starts when parents are arriving to pick up, half of it is lost. Put it in the middle of the party.
