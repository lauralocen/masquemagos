Magician for a wedding in Barcelona: where magic fits in the day

November 2026 · 6 min read

A wedding in Barcelona has one gap nobody plans for: the hour and a half of drinks in the garden of the masia, with the couple away having photographs taken and a hundred and twenty guests who do not know each other. That is where a magician fits, and it is no accident that it is the slot people book most.

The real gap in a wedding is the drinks reception

The ceremony is handled. The banquet is handled. The bar handles itself.

What nobody plans is the hour and a half after the ceremony: the couple disappear with the photographer, the guests are left standing with a glass in hand, and the usual huddles form. Your family on one side, his on the other, the university friends in a corner.

It is the longest stretch of the day and the only one where nothing happens. That is why magic works so well there: close-up magic breaks the huddles without asking anyone to do anything.

The two ways to put magic into a wedding

They are two separate services, booked separately, though some couples take both.

  • Close-up magic. The magician moves among the guests during the reception, or goes table by table during the meal. Groups of eight or ten people, magic half a metre from their faces, five minutes, then on to the next group. No stage and no microphone.
  • A celebrant with magic. Someone who officiates your wedding and who also happens to be a magician. This one needs its own explanation.

The person who officiates the wedding and is also a magician

He arrives in a suit, with a folder and a calm voice. Nobody has told the guests he is also a magician.

He officiates the ceremony properly: the readings, the vows, the rings, the pacing. And during the ceremony things keep happening that nobody can explain. The rings turn up where they were not. Something a guest wrote down before going in ends up read aloud without anyone having opened it.

That is exactly the point: they are not expecting it. A normal civil ceremony lasts twenty minutes and is forgotten; this one gets talked about over dinner and in the group chat the following week.

It is the service we get asked about most by couples who have already been to four weddings this year and do not want the fifth ceremony to be the same as the other four.

The masies of the Vallès, the Maresme and the Penedès

Most weddings in the Barcelona area are held outside the city, at masies with gardens. That shapes two things.

The drinks reception is almost always outdoors. For close-up magic that is good news: no sound system is needed, because the magician is speaking from two metres away. The only real nuisance is wind, which in the Maresme tends to pick up late in the afternoon.

The ceremony is usually in the garden. For an officiated ceremony sound is needed there: a hundred and twenty people seated outdoors cannot hear anyone without a microphone. Most masies have it; if not, we bring it.

Guests from abroad and two languages

In Barcelona it is common to have guests who do not speak Catalan, and at many weddings guests who speak neither Catalan nor Spanish.

Close-up magic solves that by itself, because it works on very few words: it happens in the hands of whoever is watching and it lands in any language. It is the one moment of the day when the cousin from London does not need anything translated.

For an officiated ceremony it does need deciding in advance. It can be done in Catalan, in Spanish or alternating, and the readings can be prepared in two languages.

What the venue needs to provide

For close-up magic, nothing. No stage, no sound, no socket, no reserved space. The magician moves.

For an officiated ceremony you need what any masia already has: sound for the voice, and a spot to stand facing the guests.

What is worth doing is talking it through with the venue or the wedding planner, so the drinks and the photographs do not collide with the magic.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should we book a magician for a wedding?

Usually between six months and a year ahead, at the same time as the other suppliers. Saturdays in May, June, September and October are the first to fill in the Barcelona area.

Can both services be booked together?

Yes: the celebrant officiates the wedding and then there is close-up magic during the drinks reception. If you want both, we give you one closed price for the two.

Which moment of the wedding works best?

The drinks reception, while the couple are with the photographer. That is the hour and a half when guests are on their feet with nothing to do. The second option is table by table during the meal.

How long is the magician at the wedding?

Close-up magic covers the drinks reception, which is around an hour. If you want it to continue through the meal, or for longer than usual, we give you the closed price before you book. An officiated ceremony lasts as long as the ceremony does.

What if some guests do not speak Spanish?

Close-up magic lands in any language, because it works on very few words. For an officiated ceremony it is decided in advance: Catalan, Spanish or alternating.

Do you need a stage or a sound system?

Not for close-up magic: the magician moves among the guests and speaks from two metres. An officiated ceremony does need sound, which most masies already have.

Which areas do you cover?

All of Catalonia, including Barcelona and its metropolitan area, and the Madrid region. If the venue is outside the city, we give you the full closed figure before you book.
