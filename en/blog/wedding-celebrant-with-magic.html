Wedding celebrant: what they actually do, and why ours is a magician

October 2026 · 5 min read

The ceremony is the only moment of the wedding when every guest is quiet and looking at the same place. And it is, almost always, the worst handled. Here is what a celebrant does, what they can and cannot do legally in Spain, and what changes when the person officiating is a magician.

What a wedding celebrant does

A celebrant is the person who officiates your ceremony: who speaks, who sets the pace, who introduces the readings and who says «you are now married». They are not the day coordinator and not the entertainer at the reception, although the three often get confused.

The work starts weeks earlier. They sit down with you, ask how you met, who is going to read, which stories can be told in front of your family and which cannot, and from that they write the entire ceremony. Then you read it and correct it, so that nothing said in front of your guests takes you by surprise.

On the day itself, the job is for nobody to notice there is a job. That the names are pronounced properly, that your grandmother can hear, that silence falls when it should and that the laugh lands where you asked for it.

Civil and symbolic ceremonies: the difference worth understanding

This confuses a lot of couples, so let us be clear about it.

The civil ceremony is the legally binding procedure. It is always officiated by a competent authority —a judge, a court registrar, the mayor or a councillor acting on their behalf— and it takes place at the courthouse or the town hall. It takes a few minutes, with a couple of witnesses, and it is usually done a few days before the wedding.

The symbolic ceremony is the one your guests experience: the one at the venue, with the chairs, the readings and the rings. It has no legal effect, and precisely because of that it can be written entirely around you. There is no compulsory wording, no minimum length and no fixed order.

The one you will remember is the second one. And that is the one we officiate.

How a personalised ceremony is written

A bespoke ceremony is not the standard one with your names dropped in. It is a different thing altogether. These are the three materials we work with:

  • Your story. How you met, what happened that night, the joke only the two of you understand. Specific works; generic moves nobody.
  • The people. Who reads, who will definitely cry, who needs to be named carefully. A good ceremony shares the spotlight without anyone noticing.
  • The tone. Some couples want people laughing from start to finish, others want emotion. Both are possible, but it has to be decided beforehand, not on the day.

There is one moment almost every couple ends up asking for: naming those who are no longer here. It is the most appreciated and the one that needs the most care. Brief, undramatic, without turning the wedding into a funeral.

And then, the magician part

This is the difference, and it is easier to understand from the guests' side.

Your guests watch a celebrant arrive. Suit, folder, calm voice. Nobody has told them he is also a magician, because nobody needs to know. The ceremony begins with complete normality.

And a few minutes in, something starts happening that does not add up. And it happens again. And again. The date of that first night turns out to be written inside an envelope a guest has been carrying in his pocket since he walked in, without knowing it. The rings arrive where they need to arrive in a way nobody can explain.

Because nobody knows there is a magician in front of them, nobody reads it as a trick. They experience it as something genuinely happening at your wedding. That is the whole difference, and it is why it is better not to tell anyone, not even your parents.

What if you do not want magic in the ceremony

Then there is none. We officiate the ceremony just the same, written and delivered just as carefully, and the magic stays for the cocktail hour or the end of the reception, if you want it at all. They are two separate services and they are booked separately.

It also works the other way round: some couples book only the cocktail-hour magic and have someone else officiating. Either way works.

Frequently asked questions

Is the ceremony you officiate legally binding?

No. We hold the symbolic ceremony, which is the one your guests experience at the venue. The legally binding civil procedure is always signed before a competent authority, usually at the courthouse or town hall a few days earlier. They are two separate things and almost every couple does both.

Who writes the ceremony?

We write it, based on a meeting with you, and then we send you the whole thing so you can read it and correct it. Nothing said in front of your guests takes you by surprise on the day.

Will our guests know you are magicians?

No, and that is the whole point. To them he is the celebrant and nothing more. That is why what happens during the ceremony is not experienced as a show, but as something genuinely taking place. You know, and it is better not to tell anyone.
