Magician for a communion in Barcelona: when to book and how it fits the banquet

November 2026 · 6 min read

The ceremony lasts forty minutes and goes fine. What comes after lasts four hours: a long banquet, twenty children in their Sunday best and a restaurant full of corridors begging to be run down. If you are organising a communion in Barcelona, that is the part you have to solve.

A handful of Saturdays for a lot of communions

Communions in Catalonia cluster into May and the first weekends of June. Almost all of them fall on a Saturday morning, straight after mass.

That leaves five or six good Saturdays for every communion in the Barcelona area. This is not a salesman exaggerating: it is calendar arithmetic.

Families who book in January or February pick their date and time. Families who call in April pick from whatever is left.

Where communions are held in Barcelona

The venue shapes the format more than the number of guests does. In the Barcelona area there are four usual settings:

  • A masia or a restaurant with a garden outside the city. The Vallès, the Maresme, the Baix Llobregat or the Penedès. There is room, there is outdoor space and the children can move.
  • A restaurant with a function room inside Barcelona. More contained and tighter on space. Here the format matters.
  • A hotel function room. Usually has its own sound system and a stage or platform.
  • A private home or a rented country house. Fewer guests and a more informal day.

What changes is not whether magic works. It works in all four. What changes is whether close-up magic or a show for everyone makes more sense.

The gap where a banquet falls apart

There is one precise moment when a communion comes undone: between the main course being cleared and the cake coming out.

Earlier is too soon, because people are still eating. After dessert is too late: they have tried the cake, they are up on their feet and they are not sitting down again. That half-hour or forty-minute gap is when the children scatter through the restaurant and the adults start checking their phones.

A show placed right there gathers everyone back at once. It is the single biggest difference between a communion people remember and one people sit through.

Close-up magic or a show: which fits your venue

Close-up magic, table by table. The magician moves through the room while food is served, stops at each table for five minutes and performs half a metre from people's faces. Nothing has to be set up and the banquet never stops. It works particularly well in a tight function room, and when there are a lot of adults with the children spread across the tables.

A show for everyone. One hour, children sitting at the front and adults behind. It needs somewhere to gather people: a room with space, a platform or the garden of the masia. This is the format that leaves the best memory when the child of the communion comes up as the assistant.

In a masia with a garden both fit: close-up magic during the drinks reception, while people are standing, and the show after the main course.

In Catalan or in Spanish

In Barcelona this matters, and almost nobody asks until the day itself.

If the children go to school in Catalan, a show in Catalan is the natural choice. If there is family from elsewhere, or guests who would not follow it, it is worth deciding in advance. The usual answer is to perform in the children's language and switch when the magician turns to the adults.

Keeping your child the centre of the day

This is what we are asked for most, and what is thought about least when the day is being planned.

The child has been the centre of attention all morning, and at the banquet that stops abruptly: the adults start talking among themselves and he is left in his suit with nothing to do.

A show where he comes up in front of everyone, something impossible happens in his own hands and the whole room applauds him gives the day back to him. He signs a card and that card turns up inside something his grandmother has had in her handbag since church. He ends up in everyone else's photos, and that is the part of the communion he remembers.

What the restaurant or masia needs to provide

For close-up magic, nothing: no reserved space, no sound system, no power socket.

For the show, you only need a spot with the magician's back covered, so the audience is not standing behind him. If the room has a sound system we use it; if not, we bring ours.

The one thing worth doing is telling the restaurant roughly when the show will be, so the cake does not arrive in the middle of it.

Frequently asked questions

When should I book a magician for a communion in Barcelona?

In January or February if you want to choose the date. Communions cluster into five or six Saturdays in May and early June, and those are the first to fill. You can still book in April, but choosing from what is left.

Which areas of Catalonia do you cover?

All of Catalonia, including Barcelona and its metropolitan area, and the Madrid region as well. Travel within the city is included in the price; if the celebration is outside it, we give you the full closed figure before you book.

Can the show be performed in Catalan?

Yes. Tell us which language you want when you ask for a quote and we confirm it before you book. It does not change the price.

How long does it last?

The show lasts one hour. Close-up magic around the tables depends on how many tables there are, usually between one hour and an hour and a half.

Can it be done in the garden of the masia?

Yes, as long as there is some shade for the audience and the ground is not loose sand or gravel. Outdoors a sound system is necessary, because the voice gets lost.

How many children does it take to be worth it?

There is no minimum. Five or six works as well as thirty; only the format changes. With few children close-up magic makes more sense, and with many, the show for everyone.
