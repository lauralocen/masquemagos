Magic in a council's summer children's programme: holiday clubs, camps and municipal pools

November 2026 · 6 min read

A council's summer children's programme has a problem the winter one does not: it is hot, the spaces are open and the children have already been in the sun for five hours. A magic show fits that context well, but not in any setting and not at any hour.

The municipal summer has three different settings

Even when they are programmed by the same department, they are not the same and are best not treated alike:

  • The holiday club or summer camp. A closed group, Monday to Friday, mixed ages and an indoor space available.
  • The municipal pool. Open to the public, wet ground, a lot of background noise and people coming and going.
  • The square or park at dusk. Open to families, with children and parents together.

The format of the show is the same in all three. What changes is where the audience goes and what has to be set up.

The holiday club: the indoor space solves everything

It is the easiest of the three, because there is almost always a gym, a dining hall or a multi-purpose room.

Indoors, mid-morning, with the children sitting on the floor, the show works exactly as it does at any other time of year. And it fills a difficult stretch: after lunch, once the morning activity is done and there are still hours to go.

With mixed ages, from five to twelve, the show is built with that in mind. The little ones take the visual part and the older ones the impossible part.

The municipal pool: what to check first

This is where most can go wrong, and nearly all of it is solved by choosing the right spot.

  • Away from the water and the wet ground. Magic props and water do not mix, and children sitting on wet ground do not stay put.
  • A shaded area, grass or a covered porch. Never the sun deck at two in the afternoon.
  • Its own sound. At a pool the background noise is constant. Without equipment nothing is heard.
  • A power point. The sound system needs a socket, or advance notice so we bring a battery.

Placed well it works very nicely, because it gathers the children who have been in the water for three hours and gives the families a break.

The square at dusk: the most rewarding format

It gives the best results in summer and draws the biggest crowd.

From seven or eight onwards the heat drops, families come out and the show is no longer competing with the sun. The children sit at the front, the parents stand behind, and far more people end up watching than were expected.

For a council it has another advantage: it fills a square without closing roads or putting up a structure.

The heat decides the time

It is the variable that shapes a summer show most, and the one most underestimated at the programming stage.

With children sitting in the sun, twenty minutes is the real limit: after that they get up by themselves, whatever the magician does. Outdoors, the slots that work are first thing in the morning and from sunset onwards.

If the activity has to be at midday, indoors is better. A gym or a multi-purpose room with fans gives a better result than a square with patchy shade.

What the space needs to provide

Very little, and we bring almost all of it.

  • A spot with the magician's back covered, so the audience is not standing behind him.
  • Firm ground: loose sand and gravel make the show harder.
  • Shade for the audience if it is outdoors.
  • A socket for the sound system, or notice so we bring a battery.

No stage or platform is needed. If the space already has a sound system we use it; if not, we bring ours.

If the activity is open, more children will turn up than expected

At an activity in a square or at the pool there is no sign-up, so the real number is not known until it starts.

What usually happens is that the estimate falls short: passers-by stay, and the parents who were going to drop the children off sit down too. For the department that is good news, but the space has to take it.

So for open activities it is better to pick somewhere that holds twice the expected crowd and where the audience area can grow backwards without anyone ending up behind the magician.

When it is booked

Earlier than people expect. Summer programme budgets are closed in spring, and in many councils the activity calendar is already decided by March or April.

Anyone looking into it in June tends to find the weekend diary already committed, which is exactly when the open activities are concentrated.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the show?

One hour. If the programme calls for shorter sessions or several performances in one day, that is agreed when the quote is prepared.

Can it be done outdoors in high summer?

Yes, with the right time slot. First thing in the morning or from sunset onwards. At midday in full sun it does not work: children will not sit for twenty minutes.

What ages is it for?

From age 5. At holiday clubs and camps ages are usually mixed up to twelve, and the show is built accordingly.

Is a stage or a sound system needed?

No stage. Sound is needed outdoors and at the pool, and we bring it; we only need a socket, or notice so we bring a battery.

Can several sessions be booked?

Yes, and that is the norm for a summer programme. The quote covers the whole set of sessions rather than one at a time.

Which areas do you cover?

All of Catalonia and the Madrid region. Travel is included in the closed price we give you.
