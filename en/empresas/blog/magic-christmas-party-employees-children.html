Christmas party for your employees' children: how to organise it with magic

November 2026 · 6 min read

More and more companies hold a December party for their employees' children. It is a gesture people remember: staff see their company looking after their family, and children discover where their parents work in a fun way. The challenge is organising it well with limited internal resources. In this guide we explain which format works, what to plan for and how magic fits in.

Why a party for families is worth it

The Christmas dinner is for the team. The children's party is for their families, and that changes how it is perceived. A few things HR teams usually mention afterwards:

  • It improves the internal atmosphere. Seeing colleagues with their children makes the office feel more human and starts conversations that never happen day to day.
  • It builds a sense of belonging. Employees value a company that thinks about their family, not just about them.
  • It is inclusive. It works for everyone and does not revolve around drinks or late nights, as other celebrations often do.

Formats that work

A Saturday morning party at the office

The simplest and most charming option. Families come to the office, children see their parent's desk, there is a snack and a main show. Two or three hours is plenty.

A party at a rented venue

If the team is large or the office is not suitable, an event space, a restaurant with a private room or a children's leisure centre solves the problem. Here the magic show becomes the centrepiece of the programme.

An outdoor family day

More common in spring or summer, but some companies hold one in December at a country house or an indoor venue. It works well combined with workshops, lunch and one main activity.

How magic fits into the programme

At a party with children aged 3 to 12, the hardest part is keeping them all focused at once. Magic does that better than almost any other activity, and it entertains the adults too, who stop looking at their phones.

  • A one-hour main show. The highlight of the party. Ideally once all the families have arrived and before the snack.
  • Children and parents taking part. The routines where a child comes up with their mum or dad are the ones everyone talks about afterwards.
  • A company touch, if you like. Your logo appearing on a card, the company name in the finale… Details that make the show yours.

What to plan for

  • Ages. Ask for the children's ages when people sign up. With that, the show is adapted so nobody is left out.
  • Space. A room where children can sit on the floor at the front and adults behind. At the office, an area cleared of desks is enough.
  • Timing. Mornings work better than afternoons: little ones are less tired.
  • Sound. If the venue has no system, we bring one.
  • Photos. Let families know if photos will be used for internal communication and ask for their consent.

When to hold it

The most requested dates are the two weekends before the school holidays. To secure your date, book several weeks ahead. January, around Epiphany, also works very well, when diaries are quieter.

Ideas to round off the party

The magic show is the centrepiece, but small touches make the party something people remember all year:

  • A festive photo corner. A simple backdrop with the company logo and a few props. Families take home a memory and internal comms gets material.
  • A snack designed for children. Small sandwiches, fruit, hot chocolate. Better simple and well served than complicated catering.
  • A small gift. It doesn't need to be expensive: a book, a game or something with the child's name works better than a generic present.
  • A letterbox for letters to Father Christmas or the Three Kings. A very simple detail little ones love.
  • A guided tour of the office. If the party is on your premises, showing them "where mum or dad works" is part of the fun.

Common mistakes

  • A party that runs too long. More than three hours with small children ends in tiredness. Short and lively is better.
  • Not asking for ages. A show designed for ten-year-olds won't work for toddlers, and vice versa. With a list of ages everything can be adjusted.
  • Forgetting the adults. Parents are there the whole time. A show they enjoy too makes all the difference.
  • Short notice. In December family diaries are full. Give at least three weeks' notice.
