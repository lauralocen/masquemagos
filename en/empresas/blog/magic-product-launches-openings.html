Magic for product launches and openings

October 2026 · 5 min read

A product launch has a problem everyone knows and nobody says out loud: people turn up out of obligation, listen to the speech and leave. Here is how magic is used to stop that happening, and which formats work at an opening.

The real problem with these events

A product launch or an opening does not fail because of the catering or the room. It fails for three very specific reasons:

  • People arrive and nobody talks. The first twenty minutes are closed groups staring at their phones, waiting for something to start.
  • The product moment is not remembered. It gets unveiled, applauded, photographed four times and forgotten before the canapés.
  • The room empties the moment the speech ends. The part where business actually gets done is precisely the part nobody stays for.

Magic does not fix all three by magic, pun intended. But it fixes two of them well and helps with the third, provided it is placed in the right spot.

Making the product appear

This is the format we are asked for most, and the one that works best when there is something physical to show. Instead of pulling off a cloth, the product appears: inside a box the audience has handled and confirmed was empty, or in a place nobody can explain how it reached.

It is built around each specific product, because it depends on size, weight and whether it can be handled. A watch, a phone or a bottle gives plenty of room. A car or an industrial machine needs a different approach, usually from a stage.

What this buys you is not the applause: it is that the moment gets filmed by fifty phones at once and circulates afterwards.

Breaking the ice on arrival

This is close-up magic, group by group, during the first half hour. The magician walks up to three people who have never met, does something that makes them react together, and moves on.

The effect that matters happens after he leaves: those three people have now spoken to each other. They have discussed what they just saw, laughed together, and one of them has asked another «which company are you with?». The magician is not there to entertain; he is there to create the excuse people need to start talking.

At an opening, where clients, suppliers, neighbours and local press are all mixed together, this format gives the best return.

Keeping people until the end

If the problem is that the room empties after the speech, the answer is not a longer speech: it is putting something afterwards that is worth staying for, and announcing it at the start.

A short stage set, fifteen or twenty minutes, placed after the formal part, changes how the room behaves. People stay, and they stay in a different mood from the one half an hour of figures leaves behind.

What can be personalised

Rather more than people assume. What we usually build in:

  • The product, the logo or the campaign line inside the routines.
  • The company name or the opening date as the payoff of an effect.
  • A routine built around the single message you want people to leave with.

For this we need to talk it through in advance, not the day before. The sooner you tell us what you are launching and to whom, the more it can be tuned.

What we need from the venue

For close-up magic, nothing: no stage, no sound, no lighting. It happens among the groups, standing, with the room however it is.

A stage set does need a clear area, a microphone and light over it. At an outdoor opening you also have to consider background noise and the natural light at the planned time, because both change the result considerably.

Frequently asked questions

Can you make our product appear?

In most cases yes, but it depends on size, weight and whether it can be handled. With hand-sized objects —a phone, a watch, a bottle, a box— there is a lot of room. With something large or delicate it has to be approached differently, usually from a stage. Tell us what it is and we will tell you what is possible.

How far in advance do we need to book?

For close-up magic with no personalisation, short notice can work. If you want the product, the logo or the campaign message built into the routines, we need weeks: that work is constructed, not improvised.

Does it work at an outdoor opening?

Yes, and in fact close-up magic is the format that holds up best outdoors because it depends on neither sound nor staging. For an outdoor stage set you need to consider background noise and the light at the planned time.
