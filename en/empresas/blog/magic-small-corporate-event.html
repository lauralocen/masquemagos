Magic for a small corporate event: what works with 20 or 30 people

November 2026 · 6 min read

Almost everything written about corporate events assumes two hundred people in a hotel. But most real corporate events are a team lunch for twenty-five people in a booked restaurant. That is a different setting, and it calls for something else.

A small event is not a big event scaled down

With two hundred people the problem is filling a stage and being visible from the back. With twenty-five the problem is the opposite: there is no back, there is no stage and everyone is three metres away.

That rules out half the formats straight away, and leaves the one that works best in these rooms.

Where they usually happen

  • A booked restaurant. A team lunch or dinner, long tables or several tables of eight.
  • The office itself. An afterwork in the big room, with the meeting table pushed aside.
  • A room rented by the hour. For a working day that ends in something less formal.
  • A terrace or a small hotel space. Usually a standing reception.

The same thing works in all four, and neither the space nor the programme has to change.

Close-up magic: the format that almost always fits

The magician moves among the guests and works for groups of six or eight at a time. Magic half a metre from their faces, five minutes, then on to the next group.

No stage, no microphone, no turning the music off and no stopping anything. For a company that matters more than it sounds: nothing has to be rearranged and nobody has to be asked to be quiet.

And it has one concrete advantage with a small group: the magician reaches every table. At an event for two hundred that is impossible; at one for twenty-five, everybody sees it close up.

Where it goes in the programme

Two slots work well and one works badly.

  • During the welcome drinks. People are on their feet, they do not know each other across departments and the usual huddles form. Magic breaks them up without putting anyone on the spot.
  • Between the main course and the coffee. The point at which table conversation has run out.
  • After the speeches or the presentation: no. If someone has just talked through the results, people need a break, not one more thing to watch.

What does not work with twenty people

A stage show. It needs distance, lighting and an audience seated facing the same way, and a team lunch has none of those.

Nor does forced participation. In a group where everyone knows each other and the boss is in the room, pulling someone up to look foolish is not funny: it is awkward and it is remembered badly. Close-up magic does not work that way, because people take part from their own chair.

What the room needs to provide

Nothing. No reserved space, no sound, no power socket, no slot in the programme.

The one thing worth doing is telling the restaurant roughly when, so it does not land in the middle of a course being served.

If there are clients in the room, not just the team

The aim changes. At a team lunch magic is there to get people mixing; at a lunch with clients it is there to make you memorable.

In that case the best approach is to start with the tables where the external guests are sitting, so the conversation afterwards starts by itself. Tell us when you book and the route is planned that way.

Teams that barely know each other

There is one case where this works particularly well: when the team knows each other less than it looks.

It happens at companies that have grown fast and have half the staff on less than a year, at two teams that have just been merged, and above all with people who work remotely and meet three times a year. At those lunches the conversation stays on work because there is nothing else to pull on.

Magic supplies that something else. Not because it is a team exercise, but because it puts six people watching the same thing and talking to each other about it thirty seconds later. Which is exactly what was not happening at that table.

What is left the next day

One detail shows up in the next day's photos: people do not photograph the magician, they photograph themselves with the signed card or the folded note they got to keep.

That is the difference between an activity booked to fill a gap and one people take away with them. And for a small company, where the event budget has to justify itself, that counts.

Frequently asked questions

How many people does it take to be worth it?

From around ten. Below that it still works, but the route is short and it usually makes more sense booked alongside something else.

Does the meal or the event have to stop?

No. Close-up magic happens while people are eating, drinking or talking. It needs no stage, no microphone and no silence.

How long is the magician there?

The route depends on how many tables or groups there are. If you want longer than usual, we say so in the quote before you book.

Can it be done in our own office?

Yes, and it is one of the places it works best. Pushing the meeting table aside is enough. No sound system or special lighting is needed.
