How much does a magician for a wedding cost? Prices, formats and what's included

September 2026 · 4 min read

It's one of the first questions we get, and it almost never has a single answer, because it depends on the format. Here's what drives the price, what range is reasonable in Spain, and what you should ask before booking.

The short answer

At Más que Magos, magic for weddings starts at 400€. That covers the most common format: close-up magic during the drinks reception, moving from group to group while guests are standing.

From there the price goes up depending on the length, the number of guests, whether you also want magic during dinner, and how far the venue is.

What makes the price go up or down

The format

An hour of close-up magic at the reception doesn't cost the same as a stage show after dinner. The first is the most booked format and the most affordable; the second needs more setup, sound, and a space where everyone can see.

Length and number of guests

A reception with 60 guests is comfortably covered in an hour. With 150 people you need more time or more than one magician so nobody misses out. This is the factor that changes the quote most.

Travel

Weddings tend to be held in country estates, farmhouses and hotels outside town. If the venue is far away or the performance ends in the small hours, travel and expenses go into the quote.

The date

Saturdays in May, June, September and October are the most requested dates of the year and the first to go. They don't make the price higher, but they do make availability tighter.

What it costs on the market

In Spain, a professional wedding magician usually falls between 400€ and 800€, depending on format and length. Below 250€ you'll normally find someone still learning the craft, and above 1,000€ you're paying for high-profile or television magicians.

It's the same pattern we explain in our article on magician prices for communions: the sweet spot is neither the cheapest nor the most expensive.

An honest warning: at a wedding there's no second chance. A cheap magician who can't read a group of guests who have been standing for three hours can turn into an awkward moment in front of the whole family. It's worth watching real footage of the magician performing before you decide.

Where in the day magic fits best

The drinks reception: the sweet spot

This is where it works best. Guests are standing, in groups, many of them strangers to each other, with time to kill while you're off taking photos. Close-up magic fills that gap and gets people mixing. We go into it in this article on close-up magic for weddings.

During dinner

Table to table, between courses. It works well at long banquets, when the meal stretches out and the energy dips. No need to stop the service or ask for silence.

After dinner

A short act before the dancing starts, or roaming magic at the bar. This is the trickiest moment: people are scattered and ready to dance, so it's best kept brief.

What to ask before booking

  • How much performing time does the price include?
  • Is travel included or charged separately?
  • Can I see footage of the magician performing, not a promo edit?
  • What do you need from the venue: sound, lighting, a particular space?
  • What happens if the wedding runs an hour late, which is normal?

A serious professional answers all of that plainly and gives you a fixed quote, not a "it depends".

  • When you'd like the magic: reception, dinner or after.
  • Whether there's a moment already planned you'd rather not touch.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a magician for a wedding cost?

At Más que Magos, wedding prices start at 400€ for close-up magic during the drinks reception. The final price depends on the length, the number of guests and travel.

How long does the performance last?

An hour is standard at a drinks reception, which is enough time to reach every group. At weddings with many guests we either extend the time or add a second magician.

Do you need a stage, sound or lighting?

Not for close-up magic. It happens among the groups or at the tables, with no setup. Only a stage show needs equipment.
